Five-minute things to do

  • Go for a short walk or stretch. Even 5 minutes of activity can cut cravings.
  • Drink a glass of water.
  • Use a fast-acting nicotine product (gum, lozenge or spray) if you have one.
  • Say to yourself: "I won't even have a single drag."
  • Keep your hands busy: wash up, clear a drawer, send a message to a friend.

        Good to know

        The NHS suggests a slow-acting patch for steady support, plus a fast-acting gum, lozenge or spray for sudden cravings.

        When cutting down before a quit date, nicotine replacement helps stop you making up for fewer cigarettes by taking more and deeper puffs.

        NHS advice is to use it for about 12 weeks, or as long as needed to stop you smoking again, and then reduce gradually.

        Follow the instructions on the pack. Ask a pharmacist or doctor which product and strength suits you, especially if pregnant, breastfeeding or living with a health condition.

        Health milestones

        Milestones count from your last cigarette. Source: NHS Better Health, "What happens when you quit?"
